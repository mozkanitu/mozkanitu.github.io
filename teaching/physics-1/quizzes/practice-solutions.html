The practice quiz

Projectile motion · the solution in symbols

A ball is thrown horizontally with speed v0 from the top of a cliff of height h. Gravity gives it a constant acceleration g, downward; air resistance is ignored.

Put the origin at the point where the ball leaves the hand, with the x-axis horizontal, in the direction of the throw, and the y-axis vertical, upward. The ground is then at y = −h, and the acceleration is ax = 0, ay = −g.

The motion

The two directions are independent. Horizontally nothing acts, so the velocity stays constant; vertically the ball falls with constant acceleration from rest:

x(t) = v0t, vx(t) = v0y(t) = −½gt², vy(t) = −gt

The parts

  1. The vertical component of the velocity just after the throw.

    The throw is horizontal, so the whole initial velocity is along x: v0x = v0 and v0y = 0. The vertical motion starts from rest, exactly as if the ball were dropped.

  2. The time to reach the ground.

    The ball lands when y(t) = −h, that is ½gt² = h:

    t = √(2h/g)

    It does not depend on v0: a ball dropped from rest at the same moment lands at the same time.

  3. The distance from the base of the cliff.

    The horizontal velocity stays v0 for the whole flight:

    x = v0t = v0√(2h/g)

  4. The vertical component of the velocity just before landing.

    Vertically the ball gains speed g each second, for a time √(2h/g):

    vy = −g√(2h/g) = −√(2gh)

    So its size is √(2gh) and it points downward. The horizontal component is still v0.

  5. The speed just before landing.

    The two components are perpendicular, so they combine like the sides of a right triangle:

    v = √(vx² + vy²) = √(v0² + 2gh)

    Adding the sizes, v0 + √(2gh), is wrong: velocity components add as vectors, not as numbers. The velocity points below the horizontal at an angle θ with tan θ = √(2gh) / v0.

    Energy gives the same speed: the ball loses potential energy mgh, so ½mv² = ½mv0² + mgh.

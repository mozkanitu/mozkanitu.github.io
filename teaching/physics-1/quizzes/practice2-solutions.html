Practice quiz 2

Projectile motion · five parts, 20 points each

A ball is kicked from level ground with speed v0 = 50 m/s at an angle θ above the horizontal, where sin θ = 0.6 and cos θ = 0.8 (θ ≈ 37°). It lands back on the same level ground. Take g = 10 m/s² and ignore air resistance.

Put the origin at the point where the ball is kicked, with the x-axis horizontal, in the direction of the kick, and the y-axis vertical, upward. The acceleration is then ax = 0, ay = −g.

The motion

The two directions are independent. Horizontally nothing acts, so the horizontal velocity stays constant; vertically the ball has the constant acceleration −g:

x(t) = (v0 cos θ) t, vx = v0 cos θy(t) = (v0 sin θ) t − ½gt², vy(t) = v0 sin θ − gt

The parts

  1. The horizontal and vertical components of the initial velocity.

    θ is measured from the horizontal, so the horizontal component goes with cos θ and the vertical one with sin θ:

    v0x = v0 cos θ = (50 m/s)(0.8) = 40 m/s, v0y = v0 sin θ = (50 m/s)(0.6) = 30 m/s

    Check: √(40² + 30²) = √2500 = 50 m/s. The choice “30 m/s and 40 m/s” swaps sine and cosine.

  2. The time to reach the highest point.

    At the highest point the ball stops rising: the vertical component of the velocity is zero (the horizontal component is not). From vy = v0 sin θ − gt = 0:

    ttop = v0 sin θ / g = (30 m/s) / (10 m/s²) = 3 s

  3. The maximum height.

    Put ttop into y(t):

    H = (v0 sin θ) ttop − ½gttop² = (v0 sin θ)² / (2g) = (30 m/s)² / (2 · 10 m/s²) = 45 m

    The same follows without the time from vy² = v0y² − 2gy, with vy = 0 at the top: 0 = 900 m²/s² − (20 m/s²) H. Forgetting the 2 gives 90 m; using the horizontal component 40 m/s gives 80 m.

  4. The total time in the air.

    The ball is back on the ground when y = 0, that is t (v0 sin θ − ½gt) = 0. One solution, t = 0, is the kick; the other is the landing:

    T = 2v0 sin θ / g = 2 · (30 m/s) / (10 m/s²) = 6 s

    On level ground the way down takes as long as the way up, so T = 2ttop.

  5. How far from the kicking point the ball lands.

    The horizontal velocity stays v0 cos θ for the whole flight:

    R = (v0 cos θ) T = (40 m/s)(6 s) = 240 m

    In symbols, R = 2v0² sin θ cos θ / g = v0² sin 2θ / g; here sin 2θ = 2(0.6)(0.8) = 0.96 and (2500 m²/s²)(0.96) / (10 m/s²) = 240 m. Using the time to the top instead of the whole flight gives 120 m; using the vertical component gives 180 m; using the full speed 50 m/s gives 300 m.
